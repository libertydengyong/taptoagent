Brave Leo Review: Private AI, No Sign Up

Tested by Jones Dwell · 2026-10-15 · 6 min read

Brave Leo is the odd one out on this site: it's not a website, it's an AI assistant living inside the Brave browser. Free to use, no account required, and — uniquely — Brave says it doesn't log your IP or use your chats for training. I tested whether the privacy promises hold up and whether Leo is good enough to be a daily AI.

What is Brave Leo?

Leo (brave.com/leo) is Brave's built-in AI assistant, available on desktop and mobile versions of the Brave browser. There's no separate app, no extension, no website to visit: you open Leo from the address bar ("Ask Leo") or the sidebar, and a chat panel slides out. It answers questions, summarizes the page you're on, analyzes PDFs and documents, writes content, translates, and helps with code.

Under the hood, Leo routes your prompts to a mix of third-party models — including Claude, Llama, and others depending on the tier — so the model behind your answer can be genuinely good. But the product story isn't the models. It's the privacy architecture: no login, no IP logging, no chat retention, no training on your data. In a landscape where every AI wants an account, Leo wants nothing.

The no-signup test

Brave's own support documentation is explicit: "no account or login is required to use Leo," and the free version needs no Brave account at all. Chats are not recorded and aren't used for model training. This is verified policy, not marketing fluff — Brave stakes its entire brand on privacy, and the Leo design reflects it.

The honest framing: the "signup" Leo asks of you is installing the Brave browser itself. Once Brave is installed, you click the Leo icon, opt in once (it's off by default until you confirm), and you're chatting. No email, no phone number, no account. If you already use Brave, Leo is essentially zero-friction.

Free limits — what does Premium buy?

The free experience is real and genuinely useful: full chat, page summarization, document analysis, code help. But free users sit behind rate limits, and those limits bite hardest during peak hours — you'll occasionally wait or get throttled when demand spikes.

  • Free: chat with default models, page/PDF summarization, no account, no logging. Rate-limited, especially at peak.
  • Leo Premium ($14.99/mo): higher rate limits, access to more capable models, and one subscription covers up to 5 devices.
  • Premium privacy design: subscription verification uses unlinkable tokens — Brave can't connect your purchase to your usage. It's a clever piece of design worth knowing about.
  • No team tier: it's a personal product. Businesses looking for managed AI should look elsewhere.

I did not find the free tier unusable — for everyday questions and page summaries it's fine. Power users who hit the ceiling regularly are the Premium audience.

Output quality

Leo's answers are as good as the model behind them, and since Brave routes to strong models (including Claude on some tiers), quality is competitive with mainstream chatbots. For general questions, writing help, and coding assistance, Leo held its own in my testing against the other free tools I've reviewed.

The standout feature is context: Leo can see the page you're on. Ask it to summarize a long article, extract key points from a PDF, or explain a confusing paragraph — and it works on the content directly. This "AI with eyes on your tab" workflow is something no standalone chatbot does as smoothly, and it's where Leo earns its keep beyond just being private.

The caveats are standard-issue AI: Leo can hallucinate, so verify anything that matters — especially technical or factual claims. And because you're not choosing the model directly (you get what your tier routes to), you can't do the explicit model-shopping that Duck.ai allows.

Best for

Privacy-first AI users: if "no account, no logs, no training" is your top criterion, Leo is the strongest free option I know. Brave's no-IP-logging stance goes further than most "private AI" claims.

Brave users: this one's obvious — if Brave is your browser, Leo is a free upgrade to your daily browsing. Summarizing articles and docs in the sidebar becomes a habit fast.

Students and researchers: summarizing papers and extracting points from PDFs without creating accounts or paying — a genuinely useful free workflow.

Limitations

The big one: you must install Brave. If you're committed to Chrome, Firefox, or Safari, Leo is not available as a website — there's no leo.ai you can visit in another browser. That's a real friction cost, and it's the reason Leo gets an 8 instead of a 9.

Other limits: free-tier throttling at peak, no model picker on the free tier (you get what the tier routes), occasional hallucinations, and no persistent memory across conversations the way a logged-in chatbot keeps history. Since Brave doesn't retain chats, each session starts fresh — privacy and memory are traded off by design.

Brave Leo vs the alternatives

Against Duck.ai: both are no-signup and privacy-forward. Duck.ai works in any browser and lets you pick your model explicitly; Leo is locked to Brave but sees your tabs and summarizes pages. Privacy purists should try both — they're complementary.

Against ChatGPT / Claude free tiers: those require accounts and log your chats (for training, unless you opt out). Leo requires nothing and logs nothing. Model depth favors the official apps; privacy favors Leo.

Against Copilot / Edge AI: Microsoft's browser AI requires a Microsoft account and sits inside Microsoft's data ecosystem. Leo is the anti-Copilot: same "AI in my browser" idea, opposite privacy model.

FAQ

Do I need an account to use Brave Leo?

No. Brave's official documentation confirms no account or login is required for the free version. Premium subscribers need an account only to verify their subscription.

Is Brave Leo really free?

Yes — full chat, page summarization, and document analysis are free. Leo Premium ($14.99/month) adds higher rate limits and more capable models.

Does Brave Leo train on my chats?

No. Brave says chats are not recorded and are not used for model training. It also doesn't log your IP when you use Leo.

Can I use Leo without installing Brave?

No — Leo only exists inside the Brave browser (desktop and mobile). There's no standalone website version.

Which AI models does Leo use?

Leo routes prompts to a mix of third-party models including Claude, Llama, and others. The exact model behind a free-tier answer isn't always user-selectable.

Brave Leo vs Duck.ai — which is more private?

Both are strong. Leo goes further on infrastructure privacy (no IP logging); Duck.ai works in any browser with explicit model choice. Try both — they're free and account-free.

Verdict

Brave Leo is the most private way to chat with AI for free — no account, no IP logs, no training on your chats, plus genuinely useful tab-aware features like page and PDF summarization. The price is installing Brave. If that's a deal you can live with, Leo is an 8/10 daily AI that asks nothing of you.
