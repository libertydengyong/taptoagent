LMArena Review: Free Frontier AI, No Sign Up

Tested by Jones Dwell · 2026-10-15 · 7 min read

LMArena is the only place on the internet where you can pit GPT against Claude against Gemini in blind battles — for free, with no account. The site formerly known as Chatbot Arena started as a UC Berkeley research project and is now the industry's standard leaderboard. I ran a dozen battles to see if it's the best free way to touch frontier AI in 2026.

What is LMArena?

LMArena (lmarena.ai — now rebranded as Arena at arena.ai, with the old domain redirecting — formerly Chatbot Arena) is a live AI benchmarking platform: you type one prompt, two anonymous models answer side by side, and you vote for the better response. The model identities are revealed only after you vote. Thousands of these votes feed a public leaderboard — the famous Elo-style Arena ranking that the whole AI industry watches.

It grew out of the LMSYS project at UC Berkeley, and the leaderboard has become the closest thing AI has to an independent consumer report. But the review-relevant fact is simpler: behind the research framing sits the most generous free-access model buffet on the web. Hundreds of models — GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Grok, experimental unreleased builds — all answerable with one prompt box.

The no-signup test

I opened lmarena.ai in a fresh browser session — no login, no email, no OAuth prompt. The battle box was right there on the landing page. I typed a prompt, both anonymous models streamed answers within seconds, I voted, and the identities were revealed. Then I did it again. And again. At no point did a signup wall appear, a counter count down, or a "create an account for more" banner slide in.

Leaderboards are fully public too: Text, Vision, Image, Video, and code/webdev arenas, all browsable without an account. In my testing there was no session limit, no rate-limit message, and no degradation designed to push registration. This is the real thing — free, account-free, unlimited for normal human use.

Free limits — is there a catch?

The honest answer: I couldn't find a hard limit. But there are structural catches you should know about, and they're not the usual freemium tricks:

  • Your prompts are research data. This is a benchmarking project — prompts and votes feed the leaderboard. Don't paste anything sensitive, and treat every prompt as public.
  • Model availability varies. Premium partners rotate; an unreleased model you fought today might be gone tomorrow. The frontier models are usually there, but the roster is a moving target.
  • Queues during launches. When a hot new model drops, everyone rushes the arena and responses slow down. It's load, not a paywall.
  • Anonymous by design. You can't pick which models fight — that's the whole point of the blind test. If you want to choose, use Duck.ai instead, where you select the model explicitly.

There's no premium tier to buy, no credits to run out of. The platform is funded as research infrastructure, which is exactly why the deal is so good — you're the data, not the customer, but you're told that up front.

Output quality: you're sampling the best

Quality here is almost tautological: the leaderboard is dominated by frontier models, so your battles are usually between GPT-tier, Claude-tier, and Gemini-tier answers. This is a better average experience than any other free tool I review — you're literally tasting whatever is #1 this week.

The blind format does something no other interface does: it strips brand loyalty from your judgment. I voted for a response I was sure was Claude, and it turned out to be a model I'd never heard of. That moment alone made the testing worthwhile. If you're trying to pick a daily-driver AI, a week of Arena battles is the cheapest possible due diligence.

Caveats: the anonymous models sometimes refuse or censor at different rates, and experimental builds can be glitchy — odd formatting, truncated answers, weird verbosity. And remember the known voting biases: longer answers win more often, pretty Markdown wins more often. Your vote counts toward a real leaderboard, so vote for what actually helped you, not what looked impressive.

Best for

Model shoppers: if you're deciding between paid subscriptions, Arena battles are the closest you can get to a test drive — free, blind, and honest.

Prompt testers: writers, developers, and researchers who want to see how different models handle the same hard prompt. One input, two outputs, zero friction.

Benchmark nerds: the leaderboards themselves are the product for some people. Watching a new model climb the rankings in real time is genuinely fun.

Students: this is the best free access to cutting-edge models in existence. No school email, no credits — just answers.

Limitations

LMArena is not a chatbot app and doesn't try to be. No conversation memory across sessions — every battle is a fresh one-shot. No chat history page, no syncing across devices, no saved prompts, no file uploads worth relying on. If you want a persistent assistant with memory, this isn't it; if you want anonymous raw model access, nothing beats it.

The research-data angle cuts both ways: everything you type contributes to a public benchmark. Assume it's read by humans at AI labs. That's a fine trade for casual prompts and a terrible one for confidential work.

Also, this isn't a casual toy. Perchance is the pick for fun AI play; LMArena is for people who care which model answers. Different energies, both free.

LMArena vs the alternatives

Against ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini free tiers: the official free tiers require accounts and cap usage; LMArena requires nothing and serves premium-grade models. But the official apps give you history, memory, and named models. Use LMArena to compare, use the official apps to live.

Against Duck.ai: Duck.ai lets you choose specific models anonymously and is the better privacy pick — no account, no training, minimal logging. LMArena is the better discovery pick — blind battles and leaderboards. I have both bookmarked and use them for different jobs.

Against Perplexity: not really competitors. Perplexity is for answers with sources; LMArena is for model comparison. You'd use LMArena to decide which model you trust, then use that model (or Perplexity) for the actual task.

FAQ

Is LMArena really free with no sign up?

Yes. I ran battles and browsed every leaderboard in a fresh browser session with no account, no email, no login of any kind. No limits surfaced during testing.

Do I need to vote to see answers?

The models respond before you vote — you read both answers, then vote, and the identities are revealed. Voting feeds the leaderboard; it's the core mechanic, but the answers come first.

Which models are on LMArena?

Hundreds, across text, vision, image, and video arenas — from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, DeepSeek, and many open-source and experimental builds. The roster changes as partners rotate.

Is LMArena the same as Chatbot Arena?

Yes — LMArena is the new name of the original Chatbot Arena / lmsys.org project. Same blind-battle concept, same leaderboard heritage.

Are my prompts private?

No. Prompts and votes are research data used to build the leaderboard. Never enter anything sensitive, personal, or proprietary.

Can I choose which models battle?

Not in battle mode — anonymity is the point. Some modes let you pick a side to play with directly, but the classic experience is fully blind.
