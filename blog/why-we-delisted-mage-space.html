Mage.space Put Up a Login Wall, So We Took It Down: The Full Story (2026)

By Jones Dwell · 2026-10-16 · 6 min read

Yesterday I re-tested every listing in our directory — all eleven of them, one by one, in a fresh browser with no accounts. Ten passed. One didn't. By the end of the day, Mage.space was off the agents list, its review page rewritten, and the commit pushed. This is the full story of that delisting: what the login wall actually looks like, why there was no grace period, and what to use instead.

What the wall looks like

Open mage.space's generator as a logged-out visitor and a dialog appears immediately: "sign in to join the Mage community," with Google, Twitter, and email options. That's not unusual — plenty of sites ask. What's unusual is that this one cannot be dismissed. No X button works, Escape does nothing, clicking the backdrop does nothing, reloading the page brings it straight back. It sits directly on top of the prompt toolbar, so generation is physically impossible without logging in.

This matters because of the distinction we draw on every review: a soft nudge ("sign in for faster generation") is fine — our rewritten Mage.space review explains the difference. A hard wall before the first image is not. The old review, written when anonymous generation worked, had to go. Keeping it up would have been a lie with our name on it.

Why there's no grace period

Our directory makes one public promise, printed right on the agents page: if a provider adds a signup wall later, it gets removed from this list. A promise with a grace period isn't a promise — it's a suggestion. The day a listing fails the re-test is the day it leaves.

There's a practical reason too. Directories rot from the top: one stale listing teaches readers that the curation is decorative, and then every other listing becomes suspect. Removing Mage.space the same day cost us one of our most popular image-generator entries. Keeping it would have cost the thing the whole site is built on.

What still works instead

The same re-test that caught Mage.space confirmed three image generators still working with no account. Raphael AI is the closest replacement — unlimited basic generation, login only speeds up the queue, free outputs carry a watermark. Craiyon remains the simplest honest option, and the Pollinations URL API still generates with no key (note: its on-site playground now asks for an account — use the direct URL). The full comparison lives in our no-signup image generator roundup.

How the re-testing works

Every listing gets opened in a clean browser profile — no cookies, no logged-in sessions, no extensions that might interfere. Then a real task: a chat conversation for chatbots, an actual image generation for generators. If any account prompt blocks the core feature, the listing fails. We run the full pass regularly because providers change their signup policies without announcement, as this week demonstrated. CAPTCHAs and bot-checks don't count as failures — they're anti-abuse plumbing, not account walls — but they do get noted.

FAQ

Did Mage.space shut down?

No. The site is live and the free tier still exists — it's just behind a mandatory login now, which disqualifies it from a no-signup directory. Different from a shutdown, same result for our readers.

Will it come back if the wall is removed?

Yes, immediately. Delisting is a status, not a grudge. If a re-test shows anonymous generation working again, the listing returns and the review gets rewritten back.

How often do you re-check listings?

On a fixed monthly schedule, plus ad-hoc passes whenever readers report a wall or a provider announces policy changes. The agents page carries the date of the last full re-verification.

A tool I liked got delisted. What now?

Check its review page — delisted reviews stay live and always name the working alternatives we verified the same day. That's the page you're least likely to find via the tool's own marketing.
